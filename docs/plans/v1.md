# First release plan

Status: product scope agreed from the owner's 2026-09-30 direction; implementation
has not started. This file is the active feature plan for v1. Future ideas live
in [future.md](future.md); completed work lives in [reference](../reference/).

## Release goal

Make Family Record deliver the original baby-book promise: turn a family's
photos, milestones, measurements, quotes, and activities into something
beautiful to revisit and keep. Recording data already works; the largest
remaining feature is synthesizing it into books.

The v1 feature scope is:
1. Configurable books from existing family records.
2. Artwork capture and browsing, with artwork available to books.
3. Annual interviews, with answers available to books.

These are the remaining feature commitments, not a claim that all release
readiness work is complete. Fix defects in existing capture, permissions,
export/import, and navigation as found. Verify the native app's status in its
own repository before promising parity.

## 1. Books — the main feature

### One composer, many subjects

Use one book workflow with presets that set useful defaults. Avoid separate
implementations for a baby book, a birthday recap, and a holiday book.

| Preset | Selection and structure |
| --- | --- |
| First year | One person, birth through first birthday, with monthly chapters and meaningful firsts. |
| Person's year | Calendar year, age year between birthdays, or rolling last twelve months; make the chosen interpretation explicit. |
| Person's story | A chosen period or full recorded life, organized into readable chapters. |
| Family history | Selected family members across a chosen period or all recorded history, with shared events shown once. |
| Recurring occasion | Halloween, Christmas, first day of school, or another theme across years, using existing tags and manually chosen records. |
| Custom book | Selected people, dates, tags, and records without needing a preset. |

Whole family history means the family's recorded history. It does not require
a family tree, video, approximate dates, or a new event-collection system.

### Selection, depth, and length are separate controls

Start with approachable presets, then expose options:

- Subject: one person, several people, or the family. Default to the owning
  household; do not automatically include linked households.
- Period: explicit dates, calendar year, age year, first year, or all history.
- Theme: tags or manual selections; semantic search may suggest candidates
  when available. A Halloween book must work without an analysis daemon.
- Content: photos, milestones and quotes, growth, activities/results, artwork,
  and interviews. Let the family turn each section on or off.
- Depth: highlights, standard, or detailed. This changes how much material is
  included and the amount of supporting detail.
- Length: a short/medium/long target with an estimated page count once the
  output format is settled. Treat the target as a budget, not a promise to
  manufacture content or truncate every section mechanically.
- Presentation: chapter organization and a small set of polished layouts.
  Decide the initial styles through prototypes, rather than committing to a
  sprawling theme editor.

A long, dense family history and a short Halloween photo book should both feel
intentional. Sparse data should produce a shorter coherent book, without empty
template chapters. Dense data should be curated rather than dumped.

### The family stays in control

1. Choose a preset and scope.
2. Generate a draft with sensible selections and a visible outline.
3. Preview the actual output. See estimated length and what was included.
4. Replace photos, adjust crops, add/remove records, edit captions and chapter
   introductions, and reorder sections.
5. Save the draft and generate the final artifact.

Persist the scope, selected record IDs, ordering, crops, and editorial text so
manual work survives reopening and regeneration. Recheck access to source
records on every read/render. Define what happens when a source is edited or
deleted: never silently substitute another photo or expose a formerly
accessible record. Mark unavailable sources and let the family resolve them.

Prefer existing quality scores, person tags, similar-photo groups, and
record relationships for defaults. Preserve meaningful milestone/event photo
attachments and avoid repeating the same shared event for each participant.
Manual selection and full-photo fallback must work when models are unavailable.

### Output format is an early decision

The owner wants multiple kinds of books and substantial control over depth
and length; the final form is not yet chosen. Explore these possibilities:

| Form | Question to settle |
| --- | --- |
| A book readable in the app | Does a chaptered, responsive reading experience deliver the intended feeling? |
| Downloadable PDF | Can it preserve the same editorial choices with polished pagination and readable photos/text? |
| Print-oriented book | What page sizes, image quality, margins, and bleed would be needed? |

Recommended first prototype: a polished first-year book and a Halloween book
from the same composition workflow, with short and detailed variants.
Compare an in-app preview with PDF pages before choosing v1's supported output
format(s). V1 must deliver at least one complete, beautiful, retainable book;
do not count a filterable timeline or raw export as completion.

Keep scope/selection/editorial choices reusable across outputs, but implement
the chosen renderer directly. No generic publishing framework, print-vendor
integration, or visual page designer is required for v1. Additional renderers
and advanced customization belong in the future backlog.

### Truth and privacy

Books synthesize recorded facts; they do not invent a narrative. Growth text
must use actual measurements and their dates rather than imply a full-year
change from incomplete records. Show actual ages and dates on comparisons;
unknown birthdays must not produce inferred ages.

AI-written prose is optional future work, not a requirement. Template-based
summaries and family-written text should stand on their own.

Books are private by default. Preview and download use existing authorization.
Creating a book does not publish a public link. Audience-sensitive fields
(such as growth details) are explicit content choices; strip precise location
metadata from generated images. Explain that a downloaded artifact can be
copied onward and cannot be revoked like an app permission.

## 2. Artwork

Preserve drawings and other creations with the creator, date, title, and an
optional description. Start with photographing or scanning artwork through
the existing photo pipeline. Evaluate a photo kind versus a structured tag;
choose the smallest representation that supports reliable filtering and export.

- Make artwork easy to add from the common entry flow.
- Show a person's artwork and let users filter it separately from ordinary photos.
- Include it in books as a meaningful page/section, with title, creator, and age
  where known.
- Support editing classification without re-uploading or losing tags,
  associations, or the original file.
- Cover export/import and deletion consistently with photos.

V1 does not need PDF/document ingestion, OCR, automatic deskewing, or a
general document-management system. Certificates/report cards photographed
as images may reuse the same path; richer document support is future work.

## 3. Annual interviews

Capture the same core questions over time so a person's changing answers
become part of their story.

- One interview belongs to a person and an actual interview date, with a
  birthday/year label. Permit late entry; do not require completing it on a birthday.
- Start with a small, stable question set (favorite food, friends, interests,
  favorite memory, what they want to be). Decide the exact questions before
  implementation; the old idea's ten questions is not a fixed requirement.
- Store each question's wording with the answer, so later prompt changes do
  not rewrite history. Permit skipped answers and draft editing.
- Browse interviews by year/age and compare answers to the same question
  across years. Preserve identity for recurring questions despite wording changes.
- Include complete interviews or selected answers in books.
- Use a concrete record if that is simpler than stretching milestones;
  include family scoping, export/import, and person merge/deletion.

Text answers are sufficient for v1. Audio/video answers and recurring
notifications are future additions.

## Delivery order and completion

1. Prototype book composition, two contrasting book types, and output formats.
   Settle the first renderer and realistic length controls.
2. Ship a complete book workflow over existing records, including saved edits,
   preview, final generation, and unavailable-source handling.
3. Add artwork capture/browsing and book integration.
4. Add interviews/comparison and book integration.
5. Validate the release journeys with sparse, dense, and linked-viewer data.

Useful acceptance checks:
- The same underlying records generate a short first-year highlight book and
  a detailed one with visibly different content and length.
- A family-history book includes several people without repeating shared events.
- Halloween across years works through tags/manual picks without model services.
- Manual selections and captions survive reopening and regeneration.
- Artwork and interview answers appear in the intended chapters.
- Missing records do not create invented dates, misleading growth summaries,
  empty filler chapters, or inaccessible content.
- Export/import preserves new records and saved editorial work; family
  isolation, source permissions, person merge, and deletion are covered.
- Review the chosen final artifact visually, including page boundaries if PDF
  is supported. Test generation failures and retries without losing the draft.

## Explicitly after v1

Video is wanted, but not a first-release dependency. Approximate dates, public
sharing, suggested event collections, richer person records, contribution
features, and advanced publishing options are also deferred. See
[future.md](future.md) for the preserved ideas and technical notes.
